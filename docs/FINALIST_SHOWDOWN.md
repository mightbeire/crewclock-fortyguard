Each finalist received the same shell, six visible agent actions, cached FortyGuard evidence, an operational map, constraint checks, a blocking human approval gate, a recomputed after-state, an evidence drawer and an approximately 75-second narrated path. ### Product-experience findings

- **ShiftShield:** The ordered stops, flexible-stop markings, locked cold-chain constraint and 168 → 94 arithmetic form the clearest complete agent loop. Its weakness is familiarity: route optimization is a crowded mental/product category.
- **CourseCorrect:** The one-block course redraw plus 2,400-person crowd makes a small spatial decision feel large. The metric requires one sentence of arithmetic context, and real route/closure decisions may be made too far ahead of useful thermal evidence.
- **Recess Relay:** The court/field/green-zone contrast and moving activity tiles make FortyGuard’s hyperlocal value exceptionally legible. Its present data credibility is lowest because cached Phoenix evidence is allocated onto a synthetic campus, pending a real campus analysis/partner.

## “Why not?” test

### ShiftShield — why not Google Maps + weather?

Google Maps can optimize travel and a weather app can describe city conditions; neither establishes which route cells differ thermally, decides which stops are operationally flexible, adds thermal overlap to the objective, preserves promises/locked deliveries, gates the change through a dispatcher, and recomputes the result. The answer is credible but not invulnerable: a routing incumbent could add a thermal cost layer. **Downgrade: minor, for incumbent adjacency.**

### CourseCorrect — why not race-planning software + a weather forecast?

Race software manages maps, registration and waves while forecasts describe regional conditions. The micro-demo compares segment-level thermal evidence, tests detour/time combinations, preserves distance/closures/aid stations, and quantifies crowd-scale overlap. The weakness is timing: major course closures are often fixed before short-horizon heat intelligence is actionable, leaving wave timing and contingency routes as the more realistic wedge. **Downgrade: material, for decision lead time.**

### Recess Relay — why not an administrator checking a weather app?

A weather app can support “inside or outside”; it cannot choose between a hot asphalt court, a field and a greener zone on the same campus, then reconcile supervision, capacity, accessibility and activity constraints. The answer is the strongest of the three conceptually. The current micro-demo still needs real campus evidence to prove that the spatial delta is reliably operationally meaningful. **Downgrade: minor-to-material, for evidence transfer.**

## Duplication reassessment

No new broad search was performed. Existing comparator research in `docs/DUPLICATION_RESEARCH.md`, `docs/COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS.md` and `docs/FORTYGUARD_OPPORTUNITY_MAP.md` was reapplied.

- **ShiftShield:** Similarity is moderate-high to route optimization, field-service optimization and heat/work planning. The exact wedge—conditional hyperlocal evidence selection, flexible-stop sequencing, human approval and verified hot-cell minutes—still feels differentiated but addable by an incumbent.
- **CourseCorrect:** Event mapping, weather monitoring and course planning are common; thermal segment optimization with participant-minute verification is less obvious. Differentiation is visual and workflow-specific, but the market is episodic.
- **Recess Relay:** Weather alerts and school scheduling exist separately. Within-campus thermal space/time reallocation remains the least obvious exact workflow, though a school operations platform could reproduce it once local thermal data is available.

### Disagreements that matter

- The enterprise judge ranks ShiftShield first; the general judge ranks it last because the interaction resembles familiar route optimization.
- The FortyGuard judge ranks Recess Relay first because within-campus variation makes generic weather visibly inadequate; the engineering judge ranks it last because the alternative space is simpler.
- The general and climate judges rank CourseCorrect first; the enterprise judge ranks it last because races are episodic and closures are planned early.
- The 0.14 spread between first and third is below the certainty justified by synthetic operational inputs. The panel does not have a consensus winner.

## Eight-day decision

### ShiftShield

**Why would we build it?** The core loop, metric and buyer are already coherent; eight days can produce the most credible end-to-end agent demo with the fewest new assumptions.

**Why would we regret it?** Judges may see “routing plus a weather weight,” and a large routing incumbent can plausibly absorb the wedge.

### CourseCorrect

**Why would we build it?** It has the strongest stage moment: one small course edit visibly affects 2,400 people and produces a large, auditable number.

**Why would we regret it?** The real decision window may not align with route/closure planning, turning a spectacular demo into an episodic contingency feature rather than a durable product.

### Recess Relay

**Why would we build it?** It explains FortyGuard necessity better than either rival and pairs a deeply human story with an instantly understandable court/field/shade swap.

**Why would we regret it?** Without a real campus analysis and school partner, the most important spatial claim remains an illustrative transfer from cached city evidence.

## Recommendation, not selection

**Recommended winner: CourseCorrect. Confidence: 61%. Runner-up: Recess Relay.**

CourseCorrect beats Recess Relay in this built showdown because its one-block route transformation makes participant scale physically visible and creates the most memorable single frame without sacrificing clarity or agent mechanics.

The strongest reason **not** to choose CourseCorrect is that course and road-closure decisions may be fixed well before useful thermal intelligence is available; the product must prove a realistic wave-time or contingency-route decision window.

This recommendation is intentionally narrow: it identifies the strongest current stage experience, not the best proven business. The human team must decide whether to optimize for demo ceiling (CourseCorrect), sponsor necessity/human story (Recess Relay), or enterprise plausibility/agent depth (ShiftShield).
