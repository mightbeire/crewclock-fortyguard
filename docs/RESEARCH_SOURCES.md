## Conflicts and limits

1. OSHA/NIOSH guidance is stronger than a simple heat-index proxy for worker safety. The dossier therefore treats FortyGuard apparent temperature and environmental profiles as planning evidence only, not compliance-grade WBGT.
2. FHWA pavement sources discuss material and compaction temperature, but do not establish that every road repair queue should be delayed or reordered because of ambient heat. That connection is classified as context-dependent.
3. FRA/Amtrak sources require operator-specific CWR plans, rail temperature and neutral-temperature context. The live satellite `rail` label and ambient heat profile are insufficient to recommend a safety patrol autonomously.
4. Commercial product pages document available functionality, not independently verified customer outcomes. They are used to establish feature overlap and adoption/integration risk, not to accept vendor performance claims.
5. The project’s live API evidence is deliberately cached. No new FortyGuard endpoint was called in either research pass.
6. HFST, bridge coating, roof coating and waterproofing specifications all preserve direct surface/substrate measurements. FortyGuard is therefore a planning and portfolio-screening input, never the sole release authority.
7. Wind is decisive in spraying, prescribed fire, cranes and some coating/roofing work, but wind is not a verified decision-grade output in the cached FortyGuard contract. Those workflows are downgraded even when their agentic need is high.
8. Public resurfacing schedules and building permits are credible schemas and locations, not proof that a record is an HFST or roof-coating work package. Demos must label any mapped treatment/crew/closure fields as synthetic unless a contract supplies them.
