## 1. ShiftShield — weighted score 9.08

**The sentence:** We are building this for delivery operations managers to solve drivers spending avoidable time in the hottest blocks.

**The stakes:** A fixed stop order can concentrate routine outdoor work in local thermal peaks even when the same deliveries can be completed differently.

**The magic:** The agent observes the route, investigates cached FortyGuard cells and peak timing, re-sequences only flexible stops, asks for approval, and verifies the new exposure proxy.

**Why FortyGuard:** A city forecast cannot distinguish adjacent paved and greener blocks or show when each local segment peaks.

**Hero metric:** Scenario-derived hot-cell minutes: **168 → 94 (−44%)**, recomputed deterministically from the same task set and cached evidence.

**3-minute demo arc**

- 0:00–0:20 — One forecast covers a city, but a driver works block by block.
- 0:20–0:40 — Show the original route crossing the hottest cells at their peak: 168 scenario minutes.
- 0:40–1:40 — The activity rail inspects seven stops, requests cached FortyGuard evidence, compares 12 sequences, and checks promise/shift constraints.
- 1:40–2:20 — Three flexible stops animate to earlier positions; a dispatcher approves the recommendation.
- 2:20–2:40 — The verifier recomputes the exact same proxy and confirms all stops/promises remain.
- 2:40–3:00 — Full-screen **−44% peak-window exposure**, clearly labelled scenario-derived.

**Visual moment:** Orange route segments peel away from the hottest hex cells and settle into a cooler, constraint-valid order while the counter falls.

**Why judges remember it:** Everyone understands a delivery route, yet the agent reveals that a city is not one temperature.

### Hostile judge test

- **Why isn't this just weather?** Weather provides a city/time forecast; the decision compares individual route cells and their local timing.
- **Why isn't this just an alert?** The output is a new, checked route order plus a recomputed result—not a warning.
- **Why do you need an agent?** It must gather asynchronous evidence, decide when evidence is sufficient, call a route comparator, preserve constraints, request approval and verify.
- **Why FortyGuard?** Remove spatial thermal evidence and the route optimizer has no defensible reason to prefer one otherwise feasible ordering.
- **Who uses this?** A dispatcher or delivery operations manager; the driver receives only an approved plan.
- **Does this happen?** Deliveries are routinely routed and resequenced; the demo claims avoidable thermal overlap, not a proven injury reduction.
- **What data is real?** Cached-live FortyGuard temperatures/provenance and public street geometry; stop operations are labelled synthetic.
- **What result is measured?** Minutes assigned to selected hot cells/windows in the deterministic scenario.
- **What if the agent is wrong?** It cannot dispatch; a human approves, and hard promises/shift constraints are checked before recommendation.
- **Why doesn't an incumbent solve it?** Routing incumbents optimize time/cost; the wedge is hyperlocal thermal evidence in the objective. This is integration innovation, not claimed uniqueness.
- **After the hackathon?** Pilot as an optional objective inside a fleet routing/TMS workflow, then validate with real telemetry and operational partners.

**Verdict:** Survives. Avoid all worker-safety and medical claims until real on-person/site measurements exist.

## 2. CourseCorrect — weighted score 8.99

**The sentence:** We are building this for race organizers to solve participants being routed through avoidable street heat.

**The stakes:** One exposed segment multiplied by thousands of participants can dominate avoidable event exposure and operational burden.

**The magic:** The agent inspects every course segment, tests route and wave-time alternatives, preserves distance/closure/aid constraints, requests approval, and verifies the delta.

**Why FortyGuard:** Generic weather cannot compare street-by-street thermal conditions along two equal-distance route alternatives.

**Hero metric:** **Participant-minutes shifted away from the hottest modeled segments**, with all scenario constraints displayed.

**3-minute demo arc**

- 0:00–0:20 — “The forecast says 37°C; the course contains many different thermal streets.”
- 0:20–0:40 — A race wave flows through one red segment and multiplies into a large participant-minute count.
- 0:40–1:40 — Agent requests cached heat evidence, compares detours and wave times, checks equal-distance/closure/aid-station constraints.
- 1:40–2:20 — One segment redraws; the race director approves the course adjustment.
- 2:20–2:40 — Verifier confirms distance tolerance and recomputes participant-minutes.
- 2:40–3:00 — Hero metric and a split-screen route before/after.

**Visual moment:** Thousands of participant dots cross the original red segment; the route bends one block and the accumulated hot-segment counter collapses.

**Why judges remember it:** The map multiplies one small street decision by an entire crowd.

### Hostile judge test

- **Just weather?** No; it makes a segment-level course/wave decision from spatially different evidence.
- **Just an alert?** No; it proposes a route/time mutation and validates hard event constraints.
- **Why an agent?** Multiple evidence, route, closure and aid-station tools must be orchestrated and the alternative verified.
- **Why FortyGuard?** It supplies the spatial distinction that makes one equal-distance segment preferable.
- **Who uses this?** Race/event director with public-safety partners retaining authority.
- **Does this happen?** Outdoor events routinely plan routes, waves, closures and aid; no claim is made that this prototype prevents illness.
- **What is real?** Cached FortyGuard and public street/route data; crowd, closure and capacity values are realistic labelled scenarios.
- **Measured result?** Segment participant-minutes under a transparent planning threshold/quantile, not clinical risk.
- **Wrong agent?** It cannot publish the course; approval and closure/distance/aid constraints block unsafe output.
- **Incumbent?** Event mapping and weather products exist; the wedge is thermal segment optimization, not claimed novelty in routing.
- **Future?** Integrate with event planning platforms and validate against real course sensors, timing and participant flow.

**Verdict:** Survives, but its real-world bar is higher because route/closure changes require substantial authority and lead time.

## 3. Recess Relay — weighted score 8.89

**The sentence:** We are building this for school administrators to solve outdoor activities happening in the hottest parts of campus.

**The stakes:** A single campus forecast hides differences between asphalt courts, fields and shaded areas used by hundreds of students.

**The magic:** The agent compares campus zones and periods, swaps only movable activities, checks supervision/capacity/accessibility, asks for approval, and verifies shifted student-minutes.

**Why FortyGuard:** City weather cannot choose among surfaces a few hundred metres apart; a campus heatmap plus surface context can.

**Hero metric:** **Student-minutes shifted out of the campus's hottest modeled zone** in a labelled timetable scenario.

**3-minute demo arc**

- 0:00–0:20 — Show one school, one forecast and three very different outdoor spaces.
- 0:20–0:40 — The existing timetable puts the largest group on the hottest court at peak time.
- 0:40–1:40 — Agent investigates heat, peak timing and surface context, then checks teacher, capacity and accessibility constraints.
- 1:40–2:20 — Two activity tiles swap locations/times; an administrator approves.
- 2:20–2:40 — Verification confirms every class still has a supervised space and recomputes student-minutes.
- 2:40–3:00 — Hero metric fills the screen beside the cooler timetable.

**Visual moment:** A bright thermal campus plan and timetable behave as one object: moving a class tile moves its crowd marker and the exposure counter immediately.

**Why judges remember it:** “One forecast, three playground temperatures” is both emotionally legible and visually obvious.

### Hostile judge test

- **Just weather?** Weather can change whether recess occurs; this changes where and when activities occur inside a thermally uneven campus.
- **Just an alert?** The result is a feasible timetable/space allocation and verified student-minute delta.
- **Why an agent?** It joins asynchronous thermal/context evidence with scheduling, capacity and supervision constraints, then handles approval and verification.
- **Why FortyGuard?** The value is spatial variation inside/around campus, not citywide temperature.
- **Who uses this?** School administrator or activities/facilities coordinator; teachers follow the approved timetable.
- **Does this happen?** Schools schedule outdoor spaces; the demo tests planning exposure only and makes no health guarantee.
- **What is real?** Cached FortyGuard evidence and public campus geometry where available; roster/capacity constraints are labelled scenarios.
- **Measured result?** Scenario student-minutes assigned to the hottest modeled campus zone/time.
- **Wrong agent?** No automatic schedule publication; supervisor, capacity, accessibility and approval gates remain.
- **Incumbent?** Timetabling and heat alerts exist separately; the wedge is combining hyperlocal campus evidence with constrained reallocation.
- **Future?** Validate with a partner school and on-campus sensors, then integrate with timetabling/parent communication tools.

**Verdict:** Survives. Use “planning exposure,” never “safe recess” or medical outcome.
