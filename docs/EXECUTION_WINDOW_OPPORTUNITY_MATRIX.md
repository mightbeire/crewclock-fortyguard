# Execution-window opportunity matrix

Research date: 2026-08-20

Decision state: evidence leader only; `FINAL_MVP_SELECTED = NO`

## Executive conclusion

The strongest refined pavement wedge is **high-friction surface treatment (HFST) installation-window verification**, not generic road repair, asphalt paving, or heat-based queue ranking. The strongest non-pavement challenger—and current evidence leader—is **bridge steel coating window verification**.

The difficult finding is that neither candidate honestly reaches 7/10 FortyGuard dependence. Both require direct substrate measurements for the final go/no-go. FortyGuard can materially improve **portfolio-scale pre-screening, persistence analysis, alternate-site search, and evidence packaging**, but it cannot certify pavement/steel temperature, surface moisture, dew-point separation, wind, material temperature, or cure completion. That boundary is central, not a footnote.

> Current best formulation: We are building this for DOT bridge-painting program managers and owner inspectors to verify coating preparation, application, and cure windows across a portfolio of scheduled bridges, because steel temperature, dew-point margin, humidity, precipitation, solar exposure, and cure persistence can make a planned mobilization noncompliant or failure-prone.

## Research method and scoring

Twenty-two distinct workflows were tested against primary specifications, public operational data, existing products, and the cached FortyGuard contract. Scores are comparative research judgments, not customer validation.

- **FG**: FortyGuard dependence, 0–10. A citywide forecast producing essentially the same decision is penalized; mandatory direct sensors cap the score.
- **Agent**: value of an agent plus deterministic tools over a threshold alert or optimizer, 0–10.
- **Data**: quality of accessible, believable demo inputs, 0–10.
- **Dup**: duplication risk, 1–5, where 5 is highest.
- **Impl**: implementation risk, 1–5, where 5 is highest.
- **Outcome**: strength of a truthfully measurable operational outcome, 0–10.
- **Buyer**: buyer/adoption plausibility, 0–10.

### Refined pavement product

> We are building this for DOT district construction engineers, owner inspectors, and HFST contractor superintendents to solve avoidable mobilization, resin-working-time, bond, and cure failures caused by scheduling an approved HFST installation at a thermally unsuitable place and time.

#### Agent loop

1. **Observe:** ingest approved HFST work packages: site geometry, planned shift, binder/product, crew, lane-closure permit, cure duration and deadline.
2. **Investigate:** retrieve the governing agency/product specification; cached/public forecast; FortyGuard hourly apparent temperature, RH, precipitation, cloud/solar and heatmap context; prior site evidence; request onsite surface-temperature/moisture reading when required.
3. **Decide:** deterministic rule engine marks each prerequisite pass/fail/unknown. Unknown is never silently converted to pass.
4. **Alternative search:** enumerate approved shifts and approved sites, not arbitrary roads.
5. **Constraint check:** deterministic optimizer checks crew, material shelf/pot constraints, lane closure, traffic windows, travel, cure-before-opening and contractual deadline.
6. **Verify:** rerun every specification check over the full preparation/application/cure interval; require field readings before final release.
7. **Human approval:** resident engineer/owner inspector approves go/no-go or site/shift swap; contractor confirms constructability.
8. **Audit:** store specification version, product, source timestamps, field readings, FortyGuard/cache provenance, alternatives rejected, rule results, recommendation and approver.

#### Responsibility boundary

| Layer | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Deterministic specification checks | Unit conversion, temperature/RH/precipitation ranges, dry-period and cure persistence, dew-point calculations if required, unknown-data gates. |
| Deterministic optimization | Feasible site/shift combinations, lane closure, crew/equipment/material and travel constraints; objective and tie-breaks. |
| FortyGuard | Portfolio-scale heat/context screen, hourly persistence, solar/cloud/RH/precipitation evidence, surface-context investigation where segmentation is interpretable. |
| Agent reasoning | Select applicable product/specification, gather heterogeneous evidence, identify unknowns/conflicts, choose investigations, explain and escalate. |
| Human | Accept field evidence and authorize start, delay or reassignment. |

## FortyGuard necessity attack

| Substitute | What it already does | Residual FortyGuard value | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| NOAA/NWS and ordinary weather APIs | Hourly gridded temperature, dew point, wind, cloud and precipitation forecasts, commonly at kilometre scale. | Finer urban heat/context screening and one-provider persistence profile. | A single isolated site often does not need FortyGuard. [P14, P15] |
| Road-weather stations/RWIS | Site-specific atmospheric and pavement measurements. | Fill spatial gaps between stations; compare a portfolio. | RWIS near the work site is stronger evidence. [P88] |

| Contractor/DOT systems | Work packages, closures, crews, approvals and inspection records. | Cross-system evidence orchestration. | Integration is the adoption bottleneck, not threshold logic. [P34–P37, P94, P95] |

**Exact better decision:** among several already-approved work packages and shifts, identify which planned site is a likely thermal/moisture outlier, determine whether the entire installation/cure interval is viable, and produce the smallest set of field checks plus a constraint-feasible swap for human approval. That is materially better than checking one city forecast. It is not a claim that FortyGuard alone authorizes work.

## Shortlist

### 1. Bridge Steel Coating Window Verifier — current evidence leader

> We are building this for DOT bridge-painting program managers and owner inspectors to solve avoidable mobilization, coating failure, rework and undocumented condition excursions across scheduled bridge work packages.

- **Trigger:** next-day/shift plan, changed forecast, failed field reading, or expiring closure.
- **Agent job:** selects the governing contract/coating rule, investigates each bridge, flags unknowns, searches other approved bridges/shifts, invokes rules and scheduling optimization, verifies the cure interval, and drafts an approval packet.
- **Why FortyGuard:** steel structures at separated, exposed urban sites can have different solar/thermal histories; hourly solar/cloud/RH/precipitation and heatmap context improve the portfolio screen.
- **Why agent:** contracts and coating systems vary; evidence spans work packages, forecasts, field meters, closures and cure periods; ambiguity must be escalated.
- **Deterministic components:** dew point, delta-T, range/persistence rules, sensor freshness, feasible assignment optimization.
- **Human approval:** owner coating inspector/resident engineer; contractor superintendent confirms means and methods.
- **Measurement:** avoided infeasible mobilizations; percent recommendations field-verified; replan lead time; rule completeness; condition-excursion and rework rate in a pilot.
- **Killer 20 seconds:** one planned bridge fails the full cure horizon; the agent shows the exact clause and swaps the same crew to a nearby approved bridge that passes all known constraints, while requiring a steel-temperature reading before release.
- **Data:** NBI locations/attributes, public bridge-painting contracts and bid costs, cached FortyGuard, NOAA/NWS; operational constraints clearly marked synthetic for demo.
- **Expansion:** tanks, marine steel, transmission structures and industrial protective coatings.

### 2. HFST Installation Window Verifier — refined pavement wedge

> We are building this for DOT construction engineers, owner inspectors and HFST contractors to solve thermally invalid installation/cure windows and expensive site mobilizations.

- **Trigger:** scheduled HFST shift or changed forecast/field reading.
- **Agent job:** resolves product/specification, screens heat and persistence, searches approved site/shift alternatives, verifies closure/crew/cure constraints, requests approval.
- **Why FortyGuard:** pavement/solar context can vary across a metro; excessive heat can reduce resin working time, while cold harms cure.
- **Why agent:** material-specific rules, uncertain field evidence and multi-site constraints exceed a single threshold.
- **Deterministic components:** product range, precipitation/dryness, working/cure interval, assignment optimization.
- **Human approval:** resident engineer/owner inspector.
- **Measurement:** invalid windows caught before mobilization; field-confirmation precision; preserved closure hours; aggregate-loss/rework trend only in a real pilot.
- **Killer 20 seconds:** a nominally sunny shift is rejected because the pavement context and full cure horizon are too hot; an evening shift at another approved curve site is verified and presented with its missing field check.
- **Data:** FHWA HFST rules; NYC resurfacing/work-zone geometry as a schema proxy; public HFST contracts where available; cached FortyGuard. Demo work-package constraints must be labeled synthetic unless sourced.
- **Expansion:** other resinous bridge-deck overlays and selected pavement preservation treatments, each as a separate rule pack.

### 3. SPF Roof Application Window Verifier

> We are building this for facility-portfolio roofing managers and contractor supervisors to solve failed or wasted SPF/coating mobilizations caused by wet roofs, excessive roof temperature, dew/rain, wind and insufficient cure windows.

- **Trigger:** next-day roof plan, forecast change or substrate reading.
- **Agent job:** resolves roof system/manufacturer limits, screens multiple buildings, checks cure persistence, proposes a building/shift swap and requests moisture/surface readings.
- **Why FortyGuard:** roofs are solar-dominated built surfaces; portfolios span different exposure and urban context.
- **Why agent:** product limits, roof access, tenant constraints, crew/equipment and cure windows differ.
- **Deterministic components:** range/dew/rain/wind rules and assignment optimization.
- **Human approval:** roofing project manager/quality-control inspector.
- **Measurement:** avoided truck rolls; approved-window utilization; field-confirmation precision; rework/warranty trend in pilot.
- **Killer 20 seconds:** the hottest roof is automatically swapped for a shaded/viable portfolio roof and the exact moisture check is issued to the crew.
- **Data:** Chicago roof permits, UFGS/GAF product rules, building footprints/locations; internal job constraints synthetic unless supplied.
- **Expansion:** elastomeric coatings, waterproofing and façade sealants.

### 4. Hot-Weather Concrete Pour Plan Verifier

> We are building this for concrete project engineers and QA teams to solve incomplete hot-weather pour plans and late replanning when evaporation, curing and resource constraints make a planned placement unsuitable.

- **Trigger:** pour-card review or updated weather/material readings.
- **Agent job:** gathers mix/pour/curing plan, invokes evaporation and thermal tools, searches start-time alternatives, checks supply/crew/closure constraints and produces an engineer review packet.
- **Why FortyGuard:** solar and urban heat screening can prioritize which planned pours need detailed onsite investigation.
- **Why agent:** project-specific plans and mitigation alternatives span many documents and resources.
- **Deterministic components:** ACI/FHWA evaporation formula or HIPERPAV, concrete/maturity calculations, critical-path constraints.
- **Human approval:** project engineer/QA and concrete contractor.
- **Measurement:** plans with complete required inputs; risky pours caught earlier; replan lead time; cracking/rework only after pilot.
- **Killer 20 seconds:** the agent refuses to calculate with missing wind/concrete temperature, obtains the field values, and verifies a night-pour alternative with the same closure.
- **Data:** FHWA manuals/software inputs and public project specs; actual pour schedule likely synthetic.
- **Expansion:** bridge decks, slabs, precast and mass-concrete weather plans.

### 5. Bridge-Deck Waterproofing Window Verifier

> We are building this for bridge-preservation engineers and owner inspectors to solve invalid primer/membrane/overlay sequences caused by a damp deck, dew-point/temperature excursions, precipitation or insufficient cure time.

- **Trigger:** scheduled deck preservation shift or failed surface check.
- **Agent job:** resolves proprietary system rules, verifies sequence-wide conditions, searches approved bridge/shift alternatives and creates an approval/audit record.
- **Why FortyGuard:** exposed bridge decks have solar-driven surface context and separated sites; persistence across primer, membrane and overlay matters.
- **Why agent:** proprietary system rules, staged sequence and closure/crew/material conflicts require heterogeneous investigation.
- **Deterministic components:** layer sequence, dry/cure intervals, ranges, assignment optimization.
- **Human approval:** owner inspector/resident engineer.
- **Measurement:** invalid sequences intercepted; avoided closure loss; field-confirmation rate; adhesion/rework in pilot.
- **Killer 20 seconds:** the first shift passes application temperature but fails the rain-free overlay interval; the agent finds and verifies a complete sequence elsewhere.
- **Data:** NBI, FHWA preservation guidance, public contracts and cached FortyGuard.
- **Expansion:** parking decks, balconies and concrete protective systems.

## Hostile review

### Bridge steel coating

1. **FortyGuard engineer:** “You do not measure steel temperature, surface moisture or wind. Why is this not NOAA plus a $300 dew-point meter?” **Survival:** it is a multi-bridge pre-screen/replan layer that explicitly consumes the meter; it dies if positioned as certification.
2. **Enterprise buyer:** “My inspector already logs conditions every four hours.” **Survival:** the wedge is avoiding the wrong mobilization and finding a compliant alternative before the inspector arrives, then preserving the existing log.
3. **Judge:** “This is weather alerts with paperwork.” **Survival:** demonstrate clause retrieval, unknown-data gating, full-cure verification and a constraint-feasible bridge swap, not a red/green alert.
4. **AI engineer:** “Rules and assignment are deterministic.” **Survival:** correct; the agent only selects evidence/rule packs, resolves conflicts, investigates unknowns and explains/escalates.
5. **Domain expert:** “The contractor controls means, enclosure and dehumidification.” **Survival:** include mitigation options and contractor confirmation; never override the owner inspector or product specification.

### HFST installation

1. **FortyGuard engineer:** “Heatmap temperature is not pavement temperature.” **Survival:** direct surface reading is mandatory; heatmap selects where/when to measure and which alternative merits verification.
2. **Enterprise buyer:** “HFST projects are episodic, not a daily network queue.” **Survival:** target district/contractor portfolios and bundle with resinous overlays later; this uncertainty materially weakens adoption.
3. **Judge:** “Why not a spreadsheet of 50–100°F?” **Survival:** product-specific high-temperature pot-life/cure, rain persistence, site swaps, closures and unknown evidence make it more than one threshold.
4. **AI engineer:** “The optimizer can do the swap.” **Survival:** yes after the agent finds the applicable rules and converts incomplete documents/readings into explicit constraints.
5. **Domain expert:** “Surface prep and mixing errors dominate failures.” **Survival:** claim only window validity and audit completeness, not total quality assurance.

### SPF roof application

1. **FortyGuard engineer:** “Satellite segmentation cannot tell membrane moisture.” **Survival:** moisture meter remains a release gate.
2. **Enterprise buyer:** “My roofer chooses the day.” **Survival:** value requires a multi-building owner/contractor portfolio; a one-roof contractor is not the buyer.
3. **Judge:** “Generic weather rescheduler.” **Survival:** show product/substrate rules, roof-temperature context, cure persistence and building swap.
4. **AI engineer:** “Manufacturer rules are structured.” **Survival:** deterministic rules are desirable; the agent handles product-document selection and missing/conflicting evidence.
5. **Domain expert:** “Overspray wind is decisive and FortyGuard wind is unverified.” **Survival:** ingest NWS/onsite wind separately; this prevents a 7+ dependence score.

### Hot-weather concrete pour

1. **FortyGuard engineer:** “You lack wind and concrete temperature.” **Survival:** FortyGuard is only a triage input; this is the candidate’s central weakness.
2. **Enterprise buyer:** “We already use HIPERPAV/evaporation charts and a weather plan.” **Survival:** agent value is completeness, alternatives and schedule/resource reconciliation, not new physics.
3. **Judge:** “The best technical component is not FortyGuard.” **Survival:** true; this candidate is impact-heavy but contest-fit weak.
4. **AI engineer:** “Do not let the LLM calculate evaporation.” **Survival:** calculations are deterministic and tested.
5. **Domain expert:** “A start-time swap can break batch-plant, finishing and closure plans.” **Survival:** those are required constraints, and the engineer approves.

### Bridge-deck waterproofing

1. **FortyGuard engineer:** “You need deck moisture and proprietary primer rules.” **Survival:** the agent requests and gates on both; FortyGuard screens the sequence.
2. **Enterprise buyer:** “These are project-specific, not enough frequency.” **Survival:** portfolio bridge-preservation programs may support it; buyer interviews are required.
3. **Judge:** “This looks like bridge coating with a different rule pack.” **Survival:** staged primer/membrane/overlay sequence is distinct, but duplication within the pitch is a real risk.
4. **AI engineer:** “Proprietary PDFs will be messy and versioned.” **Survival:** provenance/version pinning and human confirmation are mandatory.
5. **Domain expert:** “Deck preparation and moisture are more important than forecast heat.” **Survival:** agree; if alternative-window accuracy is not improved by FortyGuard in a retrospective test, kill it.

## Competitor and obviousness test

- Road-weather decision support already fuses forecast, pavement conditions, agency rules and maintenance actions; Iteris ClearPath and FHWA MDSS make generic “weather + road recommendation” highly duplicated. [P88]
- PaveCool and HIPERPAV already own key deterministic pavement/concrete calculations. [P11, P62]
- DeFelsko and Elcometer directly measure and log the coating decision variables, including steel/surface temperature, RH, dew point and delta-T, and can generate reports. They are substitutes and potential integration partners. [P89, P90]
- DTN markets hyperlocal operational weather and AI-driven planning across industries. Broad “operational weather AI” is not whitespace. [P91]
- Autodesk and Procore already log construction weather; Oracle/Primavera and Trimble already model schedules, calendars, resources and conflicts. [P92–P95]
- Existing FSM/EAM systems—ServiceNow, Salesforce, Dynamics, Maximo and Cityworks—already optimize or manage work, crews, SLAs and approvals. [P30–P37]

An incumbent could add a weather threshold cheaply. The defensible scope is therefore the **specification-aware, evidence-gated, full-interval verification and alternative-work-package approval packet**. Even that is a feature-sized wedge until customer evidence proves otherwise.

## Decision gates before any MVP choice

1. Replay at least 30 historical work windows at 5+ separated sites using cached/public weather plus real or realistically sourced field readings. Measure whether FortyGuard changes the rank versus NWS alone.
2. Obtain one real work-package schema and one full specification/coating product system from a potential user; no synthetic closure/crew fields in the validation set.
3. Measure pre-screen precision against direct substrate readings. A recommendation without a later field truth label proves nothing.
4. Confirm the buyer has multiple swappable approved work packages. If work cannot move, the product collapses to an alert/checklist.
5. Kill the chosen concept if FortyGuard does not improve an actual decision over NWS + site meter, or if the agent cannot outperform a rule form plus optimizer on investigation time/completeness.
